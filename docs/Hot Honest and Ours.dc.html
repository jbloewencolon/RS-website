The golden rule

A yes can become a no. A no needs no defense. A maybe is not a yes.

Spicy, soft, silly, serious, or all four. Name what's real.

1

Answer alone first. Contradictions allowed. Anything you'd be embarrassed to write down — write it down. That's the one.

2

Compare without selling. Your job is to understand, not to win a yes.

3

Name the gaps. A difference can be workable, uncertain, or a real incompatibility. All three are fine to say out loud.

4

Only choose what's true now. Redo it after a new partner, a new scene, a fight, a health change, or a capacity shift.

Two dials

The signal

How the conversation or the scene is going, right now. Tap where you are.

PAUSE is the one people forget to build, and it's the one that prevents most reds.

The answer

YES · want it, ask me anytime
NO · not now, not later, don't ask
MAYBE · curious, nervous, talk first
NOT YET · a yes with a timer. Say roughly when.
ASK ME AGAIN WHEN I'M BRAVER · you want it, you're scared, you'd like someone to hold the door open without pushing
Before you start

Access check

Do we have enough privacy, time, energy, sobriety, sensory comfort, emotional steadiness, and freedom to say no?

Not a tick-list — "enough" is the question. Mark each one.

Would speech, text, writing, a walk, a break, or a support person make this easier?

If the answer is no, the honest move is to reschedule. A negotiation you can't safely say no inside of isn't a negotiation.

Safety note

If honesty here could get you punished, outed, cut off financially, evicted, hurt, or retaliated against — this worksheet is not enough. Prioritise safety and outside support first.

Power, roles, and permission

Kink gets hotter when the permission is specific.

No blanket consent. Arousal is not consent. A role is not unlimited permission. Submission is not ownership. Dominance is not exemption from care.

Limits come in three flavours
HARD — non-negotiable, in play and in the relationship. No means no, not "convince me."
SOFT — no for now; maybe with the right person, mood, or amount of warning.
SQUISHY — yes in a scene, absolutely not in the kitchen on a Sunday. Or the reverse: lots of intensity in daily life, very little in play. This category is real and almost nobody names it.

Write at least two relationship hard limits. Everyone can list their kink limits. Almost nobody has written down the other kind. Don't turn up unannounced. Don't discuss me with your ex. Don't out me to anyone, ever.

Wanting something doesn't make it safe or currently feasible. High-risk activities need actual knowledge, preparation, and an emergency plan.

Aftercare isn't only for scenes

It's for hard talks, meeting the family, and the Sunday you leave.

Same menu, three different moments. What you need after a scene is rarely what you need after a fight.

Reverse aftercare: what do I need to give to feel finished? Tops need this too and rarely get asked.
Drop happens

Sub drop, top drop, and the one nobody names — the crash three months in when the sparkles wear off and you meet an actual person with opinions about the dishwasher.

How does drop show up in me? (Tearful, snappy, distant, convinced everyone hates me, deep-cleaning at midnight)
What do I need in it — and what should you not do?
Agree on a tell: how will we know it's drop and not a real problem with us?
My move when I'm certain everything is perfect forever (NRE is a drug and it signs contracts you can't afford.)
Round 7

When feelings get loud

Care for the feeling. Get curious about the story. Don't hand it the steering wheel.

The four-part untangle
EVENT — what happened, one sentence, no guessing at motives
STORY — what meaning did I give it?
FEELING / NEED — what hurts, scares me, or matters here?
REQUEST — what specific, consensual change or reassurance am I asking for?
Three questions for jealousy
Is this a broken agreement, a fear, a scarcity story, exclusion, lost status, or unequal power? (If it's the first one, skip to Round 11. That's repair, not feelings-work.)
What reassurance is actually available without controlling another person's body or relationships?
What needs to change — and what feeling needs care without changing the agreement?
A feeling deserves care. It does not automatically justify a rule.

Round 9

Three words people use interchangeably and shouldn't

Boundary

What I will do to take care of myself

Agreement

What all involved people explicitly chose

Rule

An attempt to control another person's behaviour. Ask who benefits, who pays, and whether the consent is real

Most fights labelled "boundary violations" are actually rules nobody agreed to. Sort yours.

Round 10

The buffet

formerly: the escalator

Four answers each. No explaining yet.

Rule of the buffet: more is not deeper.

Escalating isn't the same as caring more. Some of the most committed relationships on earth involve separate flats and no labels.

For each yes: which of the fridge five are you expecting it to deliver — and is there a cheaper way to get that one?

Round 11

When it breaks

An apology is the opening move. Changed behaviour is the follow-through.

When I'm hurt I — which one am I?
Forgiveness, reconciliation, continued play, and continued relationship are four separate choices. You can make any combination of them.

Verdict

The Fridge Five

Five yeses: you're in something good. Five noes: you already knew. Mixed is most people — and which ones are missing tells you exactly what conversation to have next.

So this is less like paperwork

Three games

The Green Flag Round

Before anything hard, each name three specific things the other did recently that were genuinely great. Not softening the blow — evidence you're negotiating with someone you like.

Two Truths and a Limit

Say two things you want and one you absolutely don't. The other guesses which is which. The wrong guesses are the useful part.

The Re-Order

Once a season, pull the menu out and see what's changed. Everything is renegotiable except a hard limit and a red. Put it in the calendar or it will never happen.

Closing line

This is what I want. This is what I can offer. This is what I'm not agreeing to. This is what I'm still discovering.

A compact companion to the Being the Relationship Map. The signals, limits, aftercare, and drop language come from kink negotiation practice. The no-default-escalator idea comes from relationship anarchy. The boundary/agreement/rule distinction is standard in non-monogamy practice and does more work than any other three lines here. The Fridge Five are David Richo's five A's — attention, acceptance, appreciation, affection, allowing — with the therapy voice taken off (How to Be an Adult in Relationships, Shambhala, 2021).

Hard limits and reds are never up for debate. Not a scorecard, not a contract, and not a substitute for medical, legal, therapeutic, or crisis support.
