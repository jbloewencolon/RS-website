## The brief's two errors, confirmed

- **Journal misattribution.** The brief attributes Stacy's 2003 article to *Law &
  Social Inquiry*. It is in ***Stanford Law Review***, vol. 55 (2003), pp. 2029–2045
  — confirmed via JSTOR (`stable/i252798`, the May 2003 issue) and HeinOnline
  (`hein.journals/stflr55`, section 79).
- **Two publications conflated into one.** The brief treats "2003 vs. 2005" as one
  article with a date to resolve. They are separate: the 2003 Stanford Law Review
  article above, and a shorter, differently-scoped piece — "Relational Sovereignty,"
  *Proceedings of the ASIL Annual Meeting*, vol. 99 (2005), pp. 396–400 — confirmed
  via Cambridge Core's own listing for that journal.

early theorist — four venues over roughly six years, not just the one article most

**On the Jang/Carrington/Begel paper — read this before citing it as "the" assistive-technology source.**
It is the strongest match found for what this site's own copy already asserts
("the sense borrowed from Matthew Wildcat and, independently, from work on
assistive technology" — `docs/spec/base-work-order.md:310`, dated 2026-08-07):
same phrase, same discipline, and it explicitly defines relational sovereignty as
*"the power to choose between independence and interdependence,"* which is close in
spirit to this site's own vocabulary. Its arXiv preprint (2026-03-08) predates this
site's own spec document (2026-08-07) by about five months, so it is chronologically
possible this is what "work on assistive technology" already meant. **But no source
document on this site names it, links it, or gives any detail beyond the bare
phrase** — this is a discovery made by searching, not a confirmed attribution. Two
honest options if the genealogy page cites it: name it as the strongest available
candidate and say so plainly, or ask the author directly whether this is the paper
they had in mind before publishing the connection as fact.

## What this does not settle

- **Whether Hoffman 2001 uses the exact phrase "relational sovereignty" in its body text** — needs someone with access to *Gender and Sovereignty* to check, not a website deploy.
- **Whether Jang/Carrington/Begel 2026 is in fact the paper `base-work-order.md` had in mind** — circumstantial, not confirmed. See above.
- **Whether an earlier use than 2001 exists.** Nothing found predates Hoffman 2001 (unconfirmed) or Stacy 2003 (confirmed). Absence of evidence from search is not evidence of absence — this is the boundary of what's checkable from here, not a claim that Stacy coined it outright.

## Verification method, for whoever picks this up next

Crossref (`api.crossref.org/works/<doi>` and `api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=...`)
for every DOI-bearing item — it returns publisher-submitted metadata directly, not a
search engine's paraphrase. JSTOR and Cambridge Core listing pages for the two
pre-DOI Stacy items. Nothing here was taken from a single search-result snippet
without a second, independent source confirming it.
