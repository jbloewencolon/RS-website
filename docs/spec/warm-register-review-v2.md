# The warm register — copy, placement, and critical review (v2)

**Supersedes:** the previous warm-register review.
**Changes in this version:** the Taíno terms question is resolved and converted to a build item; four new content elements are specified with placement; an information-architecture audit reassigns everything proposed across this project; Lorde and hooks are given full archive entries and a new archive group.

---

## 1. What the source text does that the framework does not

Before register: a content finding.

**The framework has no account of endings.** Thesis 13 permits relationships to change shape; principle 11 says form-change is not failure. Nothing addresses grief, how to leave well, what is owed afterward, or what it costs to have loved someone now gone. For a framework whose central claim is that refusal must be survivable, the *aftermath* of refusal is unwritten.

The source text is strongest exactly there. *The risk isn't whether you'll fall in love, it's how you'll show up when it's time to say goodbye* is a relational insight the framework does not contain.

Three further contributions: **a reason to want any of this** (the manifesto never says what the good version feels like); **first person**, which is more honest at current scale than "we demand" when fault 06 concedes there is no community; and **an erotics**, in a framework about intimacy that never mentions desire.

## 2. Draft copy

### 2.1 The invitation

> **An invitation**
>
> If we're going to be in each other's lives, I want it to be the kind of thing where we both come out more ourselves than we went in.
>
> Not improved. Not fixed. More ourselves.
>
> That means I want to know what you're growing toward, and I want to be useful to it — sometimes by helping, sometimes by getting out of the way, sometimes by saying the thing you'd rather I didn't. And I want the same back. Not because we owe each other a project, but because it's a better way to spend the time we have.
>
> It probably means falling in love a little. Maybe a lot. That's fine.
>
> It probably means one of us being ready to stop before the other one is. That's fine too, and it's going to hurt.
>
> Leaning on someone isn't failure. Most of a life is other people. The question was never whether we'd need each other — it's whether either of us could say no and still be alright.
>
> That last part isn't romantic. It's the floor. Everything soft in this rests on it.

**The final two lines are structural, not decorative.** Warmth followed by the material floor is the template for every warm passage on the site. Reversed or omitted, the page becomes a mood.

### 2.2 The principles, softened — three samples

**01 — currently "Bodily and psychological self-determination"**

> **You're not something I get to have**
>
> That sounds obvious until you notice how much of the language of love is the language of owning. My girl. My man. Mine. I'd rather have you on purpose, every time, than have you by default.
>
> *Would I still want this if it had to be chosen again tomorrow?*

**03 — "Interdependence," per D3**

> **Needing you doesn't make me smaller**
>
> Most of what keeps anyone alive is other people, and pretending otherwise is a story that mainly flatters people who've never had to need anything. Some of us can't choose it and can't leave it — a body that needs another body, a person who holds the paperwork. What matters isn't whether we lean. It's whether the person leaning has any say in the terms.
>
> *Do I have real influence over the conditions of what I give and what I get?*

**07 — "Refusal without retaliation"**

> **No should be cheap**
>
> If saying no to me makes your life worse, then your yes was never really a yes — and I don't want it. I'd rather be turned down honestly than agreed with expensively.
>
> *What would actually happen here if someone said no?*

## 3. New elements — content and placement

Four new pieces, each placed on an existing page rather than a new one.

### 3.1 "Before you bring this up" → **Practise**

Placement rationale: this is pre-tool content, and Practise already holds the pre-tool sequence (safety questions, grounding). Someone who has read the framework and wants to raise it with a partner is at the same moment in the flow.

> **Before you bring this up with someone**
>
> There's no good script, and anything that sounds like one will sound like one.
>
> A few things that help:
>
> — Bring one thing, not the framework. "I've been thinking about how we decide things" travels better than "I've been reading about relational sovereignty."
> — Don't hand someone a page and ask them to react. That's a test, and people fail tests defensively.
> — Say what you want, not what they're doing wrong. The second is an indictment even when it's accurate.
> — Expect it to go badly the first time and be fine by the third.
> — If they don't want to, that's an answer. It might not be the answer you wanted.
>
> And the one that matters more than the rest: **if you're raising this in order to get someone to agree to something they've already declined, this isn't the tool for that — and putting it in this vocabulary will make it worse, not better.**

**Risk to hold:** a script for introducing a relational audit to a reluctant partner is used by the person who needs it *and* by the person applying pressure. The final paragraph is the guard and is not optional.

### 3.2 "How this vocabulary gets used as a weapon" → **Learn** (content) + printable one-page field guide

Placement rationale: conceptual content about how language does work, so Learn. But its function is recognition under pressure, so it also needs to exist as a **print object** — consistent with the site's existing zine ethic and its "prints cleanly" commitment. Linked from the Practise gate, from Resources, and from the Invitation.

**The framing that makes this distinctive:** it is primarily about *this site's own vocabulary*. A framework that publishes a guide to how its own words get turned on people is doing something almost nobody does, and it is exactly consistent with the fault-list epistemology.

> **How this vocabulary gets used as a weapon**
>
> Every word on this site can be turned around and used on someone. That isn't a reason not to write them down. It's a reason to write down how it happens.
>
> ---
>
> **"I just want you to become your best self."**
> Sounds like care. Often is care. Also the standard vocabulary of someone renovating a person. If somebody else is holding the blueprint for who you're supposed to become, that isn't growth.
> *Whose picture of your best self is this, and were you asked?*
>
> **"That's my boundary."**
> A boundary is something you do — what you will and won't be part of. It isn't an instruction for someone else's behaviour, and it isn't a way to close a conversation about something you did.
> *Does this boundary govern them, or you?*
>
> **"I don't have the capacity for this."**
> True constantly, for everyone. Also a way to be permanently unavailable for accountability while remaining available for everything else.
> *Is there any version of later?*
>
> **"You're being anxious." "That's avoidant." "That's textbook narcissism."**
> Clinical words in a domestic argument aren't analysis. They turn a disagreement into a symptom, so it doesn't have to be answered.
> *Could this sentence be said without the diagnosis?*
>
> **"You're being possessive." "That's so amatonormative."**
> Sometimes true. Also the fastest way to make a real objection sound like a character flaw. Monogamy is a legitimate choice. Wanting to be told things is not a pathology.
> *Is the objection being answered, or reclassified?*
>
> **"I'm honouring my sovereignty."**
> This site's own word. It has never meant leaving without telling anyone, and it has never meant walking away from someone who depends on you to survive.
> *What's being honoured, and what's being avoided?*
>
> **"We need to work on this." "We've decided."**
> *Swap "we" for "you." Is the sentence still true?*
>
> **"Hold it more spaciously." "You're being so transactional."**
> Money, time, labour and safety aren't the opposite of love. They're what love runs on. A frame in which material questions are spiritually crude is a frame that benefits whoever has more.
> *Is the concrete question still on the table?*
>
> **"Everyone agrees." "The pod thinks."**
> *Has anyone said this to you individually?*
>
> **"You seemed fine with it."**
> Seeming fine is what people do when the alternative is expensive.
> *What would it have cost to not seem fine?*

**Future contribution channel.** This is the strongest candidate on the site for community input — *what did someone say to you, in this vocabulary, to avoid answering?* Park it under Contribute until there are people, per fault 06.

### 3.3 An endings tool → **Practise**

**Efficiency finding: three proposed items are the same object.** RS-029's Care Continuity Audit (step 2 of the Repair Protocol), RS-030's continuity-of-care clause, and "practising better goodbyes" are one thing in three registers. Build once.

Three parts, in this order:

**Part 1 — What lands where.** The continuity audit. Not feelings; logistics. Who holds the money, the medication, the school run, the paperwork, the lease, the visa, the pet, the phone plan. For each: who holds it after, and do they know.

**Part 2 — What you owe on the way out.** From §2.4 below.

**Part 3 — After.** Nothing to fill in. Grief content, no fields, no prompts, no completion state.

**The gate, at the top and not the bottom:**

> If leaving safely means leaving suddenly — close this and go. Nothing on this page is worth staying for. **[Resources]**

### 3.4 "On goodbyes" → **Invitation**, with Part 2 excerpted into the endings tool

> **On goodbyes**
>
> Most of what we're taught about love is about acquisition. Almost none of it is about how to put something down.
>
> So: it will probably end. Most things do, and the ones that don't end one way end another. That isn't a failure of the love, and treating it as one is how people stay years past the point of wanting to.
>
> What you owe someone at the end isn't a reason. It's:
>
> — telling them, rather than making them work it out
> — not taking back the good parts on the way out, as though loving them had been a mistake you're now correcting
> — making sure whatever they depended on you for lands somewhere before you go, if you can go safely
> — letting them grieve at their own speed, which will not be yours
>
> And if going safely means going suddenly — go. Nothing in any of this is a reason to stay.

### The continuity-of-care relocation is an improvement, not a compromise

Making it a fourteenth principle inflates the count and D3 settled on thirteen. But continuity of care is not a *new* principle — **it is the tiebreaker between two existing ones.** Principle 06 (responsibility for foreseeable effects) and principle 07 (refusal without retaliation) point in opposite directions in every caregiving scenario, and the base review flagged that conflict as the framework's sharpest operational gap.

Written as the bridge between them, it does double duty: it resolves RS-030 *and* it becomes the first worked instance of RS-009's missing adjudication rule. **Thirteen principles stands. D3 survives intact.**

### Resulting structure

Two new pages only: **Resources** and **Invitation**. Everything else lands on Home, Manifesto, Learn, Practise, Archive, Contribute, or Colophon.

### Invitation and Manifesto as a pair

The two registers should be presented as parallel, not as primary and secondary:

> Two ways in. Neither is the introduction to the other.
> **The invitation** — what this is for, and why anyone would want it.
> **The manifesto** — what it refuses, stated plainly.

**This is the site's own figure.** The Archive already uses the Two Row Wampum as layout logic: *two vessels on one river, neither steering the other, and a stretch of water that belongs to both.* Fault 05 notes the figure currently governs one section and nothing else. Two registers running parallel — neither subordinate, both carrying the same cargo — is a second place the figure genuinely applies rather than decorates. That would close half of fault 05.

**Home:** add one warm paragraph from the Invitation above the six doors. A reader who wants the acid still reaches the Manifesto in one click; a reader arriving uncertain is no longer greeted by a nav menu and four limits.

## 5. Strengths

**It reaches people the manifesto repels.** Given that the site's stated success metric is becoming unnecessary to the reader, how many readers get far enough to use it matters more than the sharpness of the prose.

**It closes the endings gap**, which is content, not tone.

**It closes the erotics gap**, and does so with sources that also close a citational one — see §7.

**It makes the framework's claim more legible, not less.** The argument is that non-domination is what makes love possible. The manifesto states it as prohibition; the warm version states it as consequence — *I don't want a yes that cost you something* — which is the same claim, landing better.

**First person is more accurate at current scale.**

**It is harder to dismiss.** The punk register lets a hostile reader file the whole thing as ideology. Warmth denies that exit.

**The field guide is a genuine contribution.** A framework that documents how its own vocabulary gets weaponised is rare, useful, and consistent with everything else the site does.

## 6. Weaknesses

### 6.1 Growth language is the native tongue of coercive control

**The primary objection, and a design constraint rather than a caveat.**

"I want the best version of you." "We're both growing." "I'm doing this because I care about who you could be." These are not adjacent to control — they are its standard vocabulary. Stark's account, on the site's shelf, describes domination operating through the micro-regulation of ordinary life, frequently presenting as concern and improvement. That is why it often isn't named as abuse.

The acidic register has an accidental protective property: it is hard to weaponise "no owners, no objects." It is very easy to weaponise "I just want you to grow."

**Requirements:** every growth formulation reciprocal and refusable on its face; the failure mode named on the page (§3.2 does this); and no aspirational language anywhere without the material question beside it (§2.1's structure as template).

**Partially mitigated by hooks.** *All About Love* argues that love and abuse cannot coexist — that when someone's behaviour is harmful, "love" is not what is happening regardless of what is said. That is a direct counter to "I'm doing this because I love you," from inside the warm register rather than against it.

### 6.2 The ephemerality ethic is not equally available

The source text's centre of gravity is graceful impermanence. As personal writing this is beautiful. As framework copy it carries a hidden requirement: **handling goodbyes gracefully presupposes that goodbyes are survivable.**

Who cannot practise it: a disabled person whose partner is their funded attendant; someone whose immigration status depends on the relationship; a carer of a dependent adult; a parent of young children; anyone who cannot afford to move out. For them, "love is worth saying goodbye for" describes a resource they don't have.

This is RS-028 and D3 arriving through tone. The ethic is class-, ability-, and dependency-inflected, and a framework centring it will read as written by and for people with exits. §3.3's Part 1 and §3.4's third bullet are the beginning of an answer, not the whole of one.

### 6.3 "Join the cause" is a purity filter in soft clothing

*If you can do this, join; otherwise, let's meet in another life.* Honest and admirable as a personal statement. As framework copy it is a membership test — and the people who handle goodbyes badly are disproportionately the traumatised, dependent, precarious, and young, which is to say the people the framework is for.

Structural echo: the manifesto's pact is fifteen first-person vows, which the base review flagged as a virtue technology producing a hierarchy of people doing relationality correctly. **The warm register does not fix that. It makes it more attractive.**

**Requirement:** invite toward practice, not membership. No "join." No "if you are able to." No implied cohort of people who have it right.

### 6.4 The soft register cannot carry the hardest content

The material condition of consent; the differentiated legal and immigration risk; the coercive-control gate; continuity of care; the differential distribution of privacy. These need declarative, unhedged prose. Attempted warmly they produce evasion or false comfort. **The soft register is a door, not a floor.**

### 6.5 Spiritual language is unfalsifiable, which cuts both ways

It reaches people the materialist register cannot, and it is a real tradition with real scholarship — Lorde most obviously. It is also a register in which material objections get dismissed as insufficiently generous. §3.2's spiritual-bypass entry is the guard.

## 7. The Taíno terms — resolved, and one build item

The provenance question raised in v1 is answered: **the author is Taíno and offers these terms as direct source.** That is precisely what the site's own attribution convention asks for. The terms carry an attribution line naming author and nation, in the same form the Archive already uses for Coulthard (Yellowknives Dene), Simpson (Kahnawà:ke Mohawk), and Kuokkanen (Sámi). Nothing further is owed and no external validation is appropriate.

**One thing does follow, and it is a build item rather than a concern.**

The Colophon's reuse terms currently state: *do not extract Indigenous cultural material without permission from the communities it belongs to; a citation on this site is not that permission.* These would be the **first Indigenous-language material the site actually holds**, which means the reuse terms now describe the site's own content rather than only other people's. So the site has to say what a reader may do with them.

Two options, both consistent:

- **Carve-out.** The general reuse grant covers the site's prose; these terms are excluded and reuse requires asking.
- **Explicit grant on stated conditions** — attribution to author and nation, no commercial use, no use as branding or aesthetic.

**And the infrastructure is already committed to.** The Learn page and fault 02 both name **Local Contexts** (TK and BC Labels) as the protocol tooling the site will follow when it holds material requiring protocol. It now holds such material. Applying a TK Label here would be the site using the apparatus it already promised to use, on its first genuine occasion — which converts an unbuilt commitment (fault 02) into a shipped one.

**`[VERIFY]`** the current Local Contexts label set and application process at localcontexts.org before implementing.

Two different holes.

## 9. What must not soften

Whatever the register, these keep their force:

1. Consent requires that refusal be materially survivable.
2. The coercive-control gate and its routing to Resources.
3. Continuity of care for dependants — including the clause that it is not available to anyone using it to keep you.
4. The differential legal, immigration, and custody risk across relationship forms.
5. That the framework does not rank relationship forms.
6. That opacity is never owed by a disabled person to the people whose support they need.
7. That a website returns no land.

Every one can be said warmly. None can be said vaguely.

## 10. One line worth keeping exactly as written

> *The risk isn't: "will you fall in love?" The risk is: "how will you show up when it's time to say goodbye?"*

The best sentence in the source text, and the framework has nothing like it. It moves relational risk from exposure to conduct — which is, in warm clothes, precisely the framework's argument about power: **the question was never whether you'd be vulnerable to someone. It was what they did with it.**
