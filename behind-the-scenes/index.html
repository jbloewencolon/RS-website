Behind the Scenes · exposed structure · about 39 minutes

How this site is made, including where it fails.

A site arguing against domination cannot keep its own workings private. What follows is the substrate, the labour, the money, the crawler policy, the reuse terms, and the current fault list.

Some of these fields are marked not yet known. That is the accurate answer at v0.2, and a blank is more honest than a placeholder.

What this is not

Four limits, stated before anything else.

Not radical individualism

It does not mean doing whatever one wants, refusing all obligations, treating people as interchangeable, or using therapeutic language to escape accountability. People are interdependent. Freedom exists through relationships, resources, care, community, and material support.

Not a synonym for relationship anarchy

Relationship anarchy is an important influence, particularly its critique of compulsory hierarchy and romantic supremacy. Relational sovereignty adds material dependency, collective responsibility, power differences, caregiving, dependants, land, and culturally specific protocols.

Not a substitute for decolonization

Interpersonal transformation does not replace Indigenous jurisdiction, land return, political self-determination, cultural resurgence, or material restitution. Decolonization is not better communication, personal healing, alternative dating, or lifestyle experimentation.

Not one universal Indigenous philosophy

Indigenous nations hold distinct laws, teachings, kinship systems, territories, languages, and protocols. Relationality, reciprocity, survivance, and sovereignty are not interchangeable spiritual principles. Sources here are named by author, nation, and intellectual tradition.

One clarification that belongs beside these four, not folded into any of them: chosen monogamy is not a lesser or unexamined form of this framework's promise, and this site does not treat non-monogamy as more evolved. What it does not protect is coercion running the other way — being pressured, guilt-tripped, or worn down into opening a relationship you did not want opened, sometimes in this exact vocabulary. Wanting one partner, on your own terms, is not a pathology this framework is trying to cure.

⌖ Safety — relationship exercises can be dangerous where coercive control or violence is present. Joint communication tools are not appropriate when one participant is violent, coercive, retaliatory, or controlling. See the notices beside each tool.

And the fifth limit, about this website

A website returns no land.

This site refuses to reproduce colonial form — no trackers, no third parties, no consent theatre, no attempt to keep you here. That is anti-colonial design, which is a real but modest achievement. It is not decolonization, and sufficiently thoughtful design never will be.

Where this work touches actual sovereignty it is jurisdictional: whose law governs a community's data, on whose territory the servers sit, who holds deletion authority, who may crawl and train. The substrate below states what is known, what is not, and where this build currently fails.

Territory this site doesn't know how to name, on the device you're reading this from, is not something a website should guess at either — native-land.ca is a starting point, not a legal record; the organisation says so itself, and its map stays a work in progress rather than a finished boundary line. Nothing from it is embedded or reproduced here — this is a link out, not a lookup built in.

The substrate

A land acknowledgement in a footer is decoration. These are the material facts of the machine you are reading on, as far as they are currently known.

Show the 13 substrate fields
Server location
Hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.) — this project's actual current host, not a future plan; see the security-headers row below for why that matters. GitHub does not publish which physical data centre or region serves a given Pages deployment, so the city, operator, and territory this field is meant to name are genuinely unstated, not merely undocumented. Moving to a host that discloses this is what RS-022/FLAG-02 would resolve.
Watershed
Not statable for the same reason as server location: the current host doesn't disclose which facility serves this site, so there is no watershed to name yet — not because nobody looked, but because the information isn't published anywhere to find.
Page weight
Ranges from about 17 KB (the Invitation) to about 128 KB (the Archive) per page, uncompressed HTML — no images, no icon fonts, no video. Three pages (Home, Practise, Contribute) additionally load a shared ~69 KB script once per browser session, cached rather than re-downloaded on every page view. This figure has drifted stale twice before — first a flat "under 60 KB" claim outgrown by the site's own content, then a range that drifted 10+ KB out of date as pages kept changing after it was written. It is now checked in the build's own test suite (checkPageWeight in scripts/check-pages.mjs) against every shipped file's actual size, so a future edit that moves this number without updating the words here fails the build instead of quietly becoming untrue a third time.
Third-party requests
Zero, with one deliberate, disclosed exception: submitting the dispatch signup form on Home or Contribute sends the address you typed to the Worker described below. No fonts, analytics, embeds, tag managers, CDNs, or ad tech — and nothing fires until you submit that one form.
Mailing-list processor
A Cloudflare Worker — self-operated, not a third-party mailing-list platform — is the one piece of this site's infrastructure that isn't purely static. It receives the two signup forms (Home, Contribute), sends the confirmation and unsubscribe emails, and writes the encrypted subscriber file. Source: worker/ in this repository.
Transactional email
Sent through Resend's API — confirmation and unsubscribe messages only, plain text, no tracking pixel, no click-tracking redirect. Resend receives an address and the text of that one message; it never receives the interests picked at signup, is never handed the subscriber list, and is never used to send anything else.
Where the list lives
A private, operator-controlled GitHub repository — separate from this public one — holding a single file encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The decryption key exists only as a Cloudflare Worker secret; it is never written to any repository, public or private. That file, taken on its own, decrypts to nothing.
What's actually stored
Per subscriber: the address they confirmed and the time they confirmed it. What they were interested in — the zine, the reading circle, a workshop — is recorded only as a site-wide count, never attached to an address. A leaked or subpoenaed copy of the file can say how many people want what; it cannot say who.
Cookies and storage
None set. Nothing is written to your device, so nothing needs consenting to and there is no banner to recite at you.
Security headers
A Content-Security-Policy on every page, machine-checked in CI (a build fails if any page loads a script, stylesheet, or CSS import/url() from off this domain — outbound prose links are exempt on purpose, that's citation, not a subresource load). Each page's policy is only as permissive as that specific page needs: the four reading pages allow no script execution beyond their own same-origin file, Practise/Home/Contribute need a narrow eval allowance for the page-template runtime they still run on, and only Home and Contribute's connect-src includes the dispatch Worker's origin. Delivered as a <meta> tag, not a response header, because GitHub Pages — this site's current host — cannot set response headers. That has one real consequence: frame-ancestors (clickjacking protection) cannot be delivered via <meta> at all, by the CSP specification itself, so this site currently has none. Fault below.
What is logged
Whatever GitHub Pages, this project's current host, logs by default — not yet audited or reduced, and not yet stated here in specific terms. This project inherited a host before confirming its logging practices meet the minimisation this page states as a goal, which is backwards, and this row says so rather than implying the sequencing went the other way.
Build
Every reading page — Manifesto, Invitation, Learn, Archive, Resources, this page — is generated from plain data files into plain HTML by Hugo (a single static binary, no dependency tree): the output is committed and is what ships, no runtime, no framework, opens offline from a saved file. The generator exists for whoever maintains this, not for whoever reads it. Home, Practise, and Contribute keep the dc-runtime instead, since each carries live interactive logic (forms, a stateful tool) Hugo's static output can't hold. Archive keeps one small script of its own, archive-filter.js, for its filter-by-tag convenience — the one piece of client-side JavaScript on a Hugo-generated page, and a plain progressive enhancement: every entry renders regardless, the script only ever narrows what is already there.
Durability
Met, for the reading pages — all four now render and read fully with scripting off, not asserted but verified against a real headless browser with JavaScript disabled. Practise is the one page that still needs its runtime, deliberately: it is interactive by nature, not a reading page.

Type, and why it changed

Version 0.1 loaded three faces from a font CDN. That disclosed every reader's address to a third party on page load, while the footer claimed no third parties. The claim was false, so the fonts went.

What replaced them is a system stack: no request leaves your device for type, and the faces already on your machine carry far wider orthographic coverage than the display faces did. The identity is quieter. That is the correct trade.

Reading · Georgia, Iowan Old Style, Palatino Linotype, Palatino
Structure · ui-monospace, SF Mono, Cascadia Mono, Menlo, Consolas

Coverage, checked not assumed

A font that turns a nation's name into boxes makes a people unwritable in their own name. Every diacritic this site carries was measured against the notdef box in each stack, rather than trusted.

  • ok ʔ U+0294 glottal stop — Skarù·ręʔ
  • ok · U+00B7 middle dot — Skarù·ręʔ
  • ok à ù ê î é precomposed diacritics — Kahnawà:ke, nêhiyawêwin
  • ok ę ř ogonek, caron
  • ok ʼ U+02BC modifier apostrophe — Kwakwaka'wakw
  • ok x̂ x + U+0302 combining circumflex — Unangax̂, rendered live in the mono stack (Archive's by-line). Previously flagged at-risk because advance-width measurement was inconclusive on some faces; the check now compares rendered pixels instead and gives a definite answer. Confirmed present on the one platform re-verified this session.
  • ok ★ ⌖ ▪ □ — interface marks

Re-measured 2026 with a pixel-comparison check: the previous method compared advance widths against the .notdef box, which cannot detect a missing glyph in a monospace face at all — every character there shares one fixed advance width by design, present or not. That was a real gap in "checked, not assumed," not a platform-coverage gap, and it's fixed now. Confirmed clean this session on Chrome / Linux, the one platform available to re-verify it on. Windows, macOS, iOS, and Android — and Safari, Firefox, and Edge — have not been independently re-checked and are a known gap. Results can vary by platform, which is why each stack names several faces. The check itself is viewable and re-runnable.

Opacity as governance, not as a button

What this site refuses to the crawler.

Discovery by a third party is not a condition of existing. A publisher can decline the index, and this one does, selectively. The policy is a real file, not a claim: read robots.txt.

indexed

Manifesto, Learn, Archive, Behind the Scenes

Public reading material. Find it, index it, pass it on. Refusing discovery for its own sake would just be obscurity.

disallowed

Practise

What a person marks about their own relationships is not material for a register, a snapshot, or a training set — even though nothing they type ever leaves their browser.

disallowed

Contribute

Contributor material is reachable by relationship and request, governed by the terms each contributor chose, not by discovery.

refused

Training crawlers

GPTBot, CCBot, Google-Extended, ClaudeBot and their kin are disallowed site-wide. This request has no technical force; it is stated so that ignoring it is a choice on the record.

allowed, with limits

Retrieval crawlers

Training and retrieval are different acts. A crawler fetching this page to answer a person's question, and citing it back, may read the public pages — it is refused the workshop and the contribution page like anyone else. Refusing to become training data is not the same as refusing to be found.

⌖ The caveat that governs all of this: opacity is owed to the crawler and the market, never to the disabled reader. Nothing here is hidden from assistive technology, and no semantic structure is removed to look less legible. Withhold from extraction; disclose fully to those who need access to participate.

Protocol-gated access — where a community, not this project, sets who may see what and under which relationship — is not built yet. When it is, it will follow work that already exists rather than a scheme invented here: Mukurtu CMS, built with the Warumungu community; Local Contexts TK and BC Labels; and the CARE Principles for Indigenous data governance.

Reuse terms, version 0.2

Terms that only bind the reader are not terms; they are imposed law. These bind the project too.

  • Fork, translate, reprint, adapt, and teach from this site freely, with attribution travelling with the fragment.
  • Do not extract Indigenous cultural material without permission from the communities it belongs to. A citation on this site is not that permission.
  • Contributors keep their own work. The project holds only the licence each contributor chose, and holds it for only as long as they say.
  • No unilateral amendment. Terms carry a version; changes are logged with the prior wording left legible, and never apply retroactively.
  • No arbitration clause, no forum selection, no waiver of your own courts.
  • No sale of the archive. If the project closes, contributors are notified and material is returned or deleted on their terms.
  • This site claims no jurisdiction over anyone who reads it.

⌖ No unilateral amendment. These terms carry a version number; changes are recorded with the previous wording left legible, in the project's version history. A change cannot apply retroactively to material already contributed under earlier terms.

The fault list

Known and unfixed, published rather than quietly carried. Naming them is not absolution; it is the work list.

  1. 01

    The server has no declared territory yet

    The site is hosted — GitHub Pages, live at this domain — but the most material fields in the colophon (server location, watershed) are blank for a different reason than this fault used to say: not because nothing exists yet, but because the current host doesn't disclose which facility it uses. Naming the actual machine's location and watershed, on a host that can name them, is a launch condition this project has not met, not an optional extra.

  2. 02

    Protocol-gated access is not built

    Opacity is currently expressed as a crawler policy and as honest absence. Community-defined access rules — the Mukurtu and TK Label model — are not implemented, so no material that genuinely needs protocol is held here yet.

  3. 03

    There is no community, so governance is a promise

    An editorial charter, advisory group, and appeals process are described in the future tense on the Contribute page. Until people exist to hold them, they are intentions, and this site says so rather than implying otherwise.

  4. 04

    No clickjacking protection

    The CSP is delivered as a <meta> tag because the current host cannot set response headers — and frame-ancestors, the directive that would stop this site being framed inside someone else's page, is one of a small number the CSP specification refuses to honour outside a real header, on any browser, no matter how it's written. There is currently no way to close this gap without changing host. Same root cause as fault 01.

Labour and money

Who has worked on this

At v0.2: one unpaid person, writing and building. Every future contributor, translator, reviewer, and maintainer will be named here individually, not aggregated into a credit line.

What is paid

Nothing yet, which is why the archive's source notes are marked provisional. Indigenous review, disability-access review, and facilitation are paid work; until there is money for them, they are listed as owed rather than described as done.

What it costs to run

Static pages, no database, no analytics, no CDN. Once hosted, the running cost and its funding source will be published as a figure here.

Sources this site is built from

Four research documents and a manifesto held by the project, and the writers named in the archive — who are owed the credit, and to whom this site is only a door.

The limit

A website returns no land.

Everything on this page is more accurately called anti-colonial design: a refusal to reproduce colonial form. That is a real but modest achievement, and it is not the repatriation of territory or life. Sufficiently thoughtful design is not decolonization.

The substrate is not exited either — a naming registry, two browser engines, corporate networks, and a physical data centre remain. Where this work touches actual sovereignty it is jurisdictional: whose law governs a community's data, on whose territory the servers sit, who holds deletion authority, who may crawl and train. That is a question about land, not layout.
