The Reading Room

A site that hands you the writing, and refuses to keep you.

This document both describes the website and is built the way the website should be built. The medium is the argument. These margin marks name what the page is doing as you read it.

00 — how to read this

An aesthetic can be described, or it can be practised.

This page practises it.

The relational-sovereignty framework rejects domination, ranking, surveillance, and possession. A reading site built to spread those ideas cannot then rank its texts, farm your attention, or trap you inside itself — that would reproduce the colony it names. So the plan below is written into the page: a polycentric layout with no central command, a library shaped as a woven map rather than a list, structure left visible and vulnerable material kept opaque, and a palette that means something. What follows is the site's goals, its structural and aesthetic elements, and its strategy — beginning, as the site itself would, with a refusal.

the threshold

A Relational Sovereignty Manifesto

This is not a dating system. This is not a softer vocabulary for control. This is not colonial possession painted in the colours of consent. This is a refusal.

A refusal of the relationship as property. A refusal of the lover as territory. A refusal of the child as extension. A refusal of the friend as resource. A refusal of the caregiver as servant. A refusal of love organized like an empire.
I do not own you. You do not own me. We are still responsible to each other. That is the contradiction. Live inside it.

  1. Love is not a land claim

    Colonialism drew borders around land, then around bodies, then entered the home: my wife, my children, my people. Mine. The grammar was never innocent. No one becomes your property because they kissed you, married you, raised you, or once promised to stay.Love is participation, not annexation.

  2. Burn the default settings

    The colonial relationship arrives preassembled — couple first, blood first, men command, women care, parents know, children obey. We will not mistake tradition for consent, or familiarity for freedom. Every relationship must answer for itself: who made its rules, who benefits, who can leave without losing everything?No rule is sacred just because it is old.

  3. Consent is not a colonial permit

    Consent is not a visa granting permanent access to a body, a life, a future. It is alive. It can be withdrawn, renegotiated, given in one part of a relationship and not another. Yes to touch is not yes to sex. Yes yesterday is not yes forever. Consent without the ability to refuse is theatre.Ask who can say no — and what saying no will cost.

  4. Autonomy is not isolation

    The empire loves the ruler who needs nobody and the worker who receives nothing. We reject both. The problem is not dependence — we all depend — but dependence controlled by someone else. Sovereignty is power over the conditions of our interdependence.Independence is not the price of dignity.

  5. No hierarchy without accountability

    Not every relationship is symmetrical; parent and child, teacher and student, carer and cared-for hold different power. Difference is real; domination is not inevitable. Authority must be named, limited, and contestable. Power that cannot be challenged becomes rule.No secret thrones.

  6. Build constellations, not pyramids

    Romance is not automatically the highest relationship. Marriage is not automatically deeper than friendship. Blood is not automatically safer than chosen kin. Value each bond by what it actually holds — trust, care, history, risk, labour — not by the rank the state, church, or market assigned it.Build constellations, not pyramids.

  7. Make the invisible labour visible

    Colonial relationships survive on hidden work. Someone remembers the appointments, calms the conflict, explains the racism, absorbs the anger, disappears so the peace can appear. Name it. Count it. Redistribute it. Rest is relational justice.Care is work. Care is power. Care must not become a caste system.

  8. Practise the right to opacity

    You are not entitled to total access — not to every memory, password, thought, or sacred teaching. Colonial power demands that people become completely legible: explain, confess, classify. We refuse. Intimacy does not require total exposure; trust does not require surveillance.Some parts of a person are not public property.

  9. Refusal is a relational act

    No. Not like this. Not today. This does not belong to you. I am changing the agreement. Refusal is not the opposite of relationship — sometimes it is how a bond becomes honest, or how it ends because it has become a machine for harm. Do not call obedience loyalty, fear commitment, or silence peace.A relationship that cannot survive a boundary was surviving on access, not love.

  10. No liberation without material conditions

    You cannot decolonize a relationship through language alone. A beautiful agreement means little when one person controls the housing, the money, the healthcare, the immigration status, the access to children. Emotional freedom requires material alternatives. Mutual aid is part of consent.A person is not free to leave when leaving means disappearing.

  11. Relationships are not private empires

    What happens between two people does not always stay between two people; children, friends, communities, care networks, and land absorb the consequences. We reject the excuse that anything is ethical because consenting adults arranged it behind closed doors.No private freedom built on public damage.

  12. Repair is not rebranding

    An apology is not repair. A therapy word is not repair. Repair requires changed behaviour, time, returning what was taken, and surrendering the benefits produced by harm. Nobody owes reconciliation to the person who harmed them. Nobody owes access as proof of healing.Accountability without consequences is public relations.

  13. Let relationships change shape

    The colonial imagination loves fixed categories — friend, partner, spouse, ex — and demands every bond stay recognizable. We refuse the museum. Friendship may become love; love may become kinship; marriage may become co-parenting. An ending does not make the past false.We do not have to destroy what was real to accept what is over.

  14. Survivance, not "no future"

    Colonial relationships reproduce by insisting there is no alternative: this is just how men are, just what marriage is, just human nature. Lies. We are not here only to survive damaged forms of connection. We are here to regenerate it — kinship after displacement, trust after betrayal, family beyond blood. We do not worship destruction; we destroy what prevents life, then we build.There is a future, and it will be relational.

  15. Do it yourself, but never alone

    Do not wait for the state to recognize your relationships, the market to package your liberation, or an app to optimize your intimacy. Write your agreements. Build your care networks. Make caregiving public. Make accountability ordinary. Print the zine. Walk away when necessary; return when invited.DIY does not mean solitary. It means we stop waiting for the institutions that harmed us to teach us how to love.

Our demands

Relationships without ownership. Consent with material alternatives. Care without compulsory self-sacrifice. Authority that can be questioned. Privacy without suspicion. Commitment without possession. Community without forced conformity. Accountability without carceral revenge. Kinship beyond blood and romance. The right to change, to refuse, to remain opaque, to leave — and to return differently.

The pact

I will not treat love as ownership. I will not confuse access with intimacy. I will not demand transparency as proof of loyalty. I will examine the power I hold and make hidden labour visible. I will respect refusal, and I will not use care to create debt. I will not call domination tradition, possession commitment, or silence harmony. I will help build the conditions in which others can freely choose. I will remain responsible for the consequences of my freedom. I will practise connection without conquest — I will love without colonizing.

No masters. No property. No disposable people. No colonies in our relationships.

Element 5 — bricolage as consent: this manifesto is meant to be photocopied, forked, and passed on, with attribution travelling with it — DIY, not open-appropriated. It is the site's loud, exposed surface; the cited depth lives in the rooms below.

01 — the role of the manifesto

A threshold, not the whole house.

The manifesto is the site's front door: the fast, felt, punk-register refusal that recruits before the citations arrive. Scholarship establishes the ideas; the manifesto makes the stakes visceral and portable — a thing you can print, quote, and read aloud at an assembly. It carries the framework's core moves in miniature: opacity (§8), material conditions (§10), repair (§12), and survivance against "no future" (§14).

But it cannot be the entire site. A page that is only refusal and only surface collapses into the punk failure mode — pure negation, total exposure, no accountability. So structurally the manifesto is one of two registers, held in relation like the Two Row Wampum: the loud refusal (this dark band) running parallel to the slow, cited, relational reading rooms (the paper bands). It opens onto them; it does not replace them.

Element 4 — dual regime: notice the two grounds. The refusal is set on deep ink and turned up loud; the reading and reasoning sit on paper and are kept quiet. Register is doing structural work.

02 — goals

What the site is for.

  1. Get people actually reading the primary texts

    Move readers from a skim of a concept into sustained contact with the writing itself — Indigenous sovereignty and relationality, feminist relational autonomy, care ethics, disability justice, relationship anarchy.

  2. Let the ideas be felt before they are footnoted

    The refusal lands first; the citations follow. Affect and rigor are sequenced, not opposed.

  3. Model non-domination in the reading experience itself

    No attention farming, no ranking of texts, no surveillance of readers. The experience obeys the politics.

  4. Protect the vulnerable and the sacred

    Some material is gated, opaque, or simply absent — by design, and by the consent of the communities it belongs to.

  5. Circulate with consent and reciprocity

    Everything is attributed; much is printable and forkable; none of it is extractable. The DIY ethic, without the appropriation.

  6. Point outward, not inward

    The site is a threshold to the writers, publishers, and communities — a doorway, not a destination that keeps you. Its success is measured by how quickly it becomes unnecessary.

03 — structural & aesthetic elements

Twelve principles, each built into the page.

These are carried over from the aesthetic of relational sovereignty. For each, what it means as a website — and, where the page can, a mark showing it working right now.

  1. Prefigurative, non-dominating composition

    Polycentric layout: no single triumphant hero, no funnel toward one action. Reading paths branch; nothing is forced to the top.

    There is no giant "Start reading" button demanding your click. That absence is the principle.

  2. Relation as the primary unit

    The library is a woven map, not a list. Texts are shown by how they hold one another; the connective thread, not the individual title, is the thing to look at.

    Enacted below in the constellation, and by the green thread running down this page's left margin.

  3. Differentiation — connection without dissolution

    Two reading tracks run in parallel — Sovereignty · Refusal · Relation and Aesthetics · Form · Practice — connected but neither steering the other, on the model of the Two Row Wampum.

  4. Dual regime — exposed structure, sheltered interior

    The site shows its own scaffolding (provenance, versioning, source view) while veiling sacred or vulnerable material. Total legibility is treated as a mode of domination, not a virtue.

    A demonstration of opacity

    This paragraph is withheld until you choose. In a colonial reading economy, everything is made legible on demand — explain, confess, classify. Relational sovereignty keeps a right to opacity: some things are shown only by consent, and some are never shown at all. You revealed this. On the live site, some blocks would stay opaque no matter what you clicked.

  5. Bricolage as consent, not shock

    Readers assemble their own path and print their own zine from modular pieces — self-authored, DIY — with attribution always travelling with the fragment. Punk's method, stripped of provocation for its own sake.

  6. Consented, reciprocal circulation

    Every text carries provenance, attribution, and a reuse note; some material is not reproducible at all. Anti-market and anti-paywall, but not extractable.

    Enacted below: each constellation node opens a provenance card, not a "buy" link.

  7. Survivance & futurity, not negation

    No "no future." Reading paths are cyclical and generative, oriented toward regeneration; ambient motion drifts and returns rather than detonating.

  8. Repair as visible form

    Corrections and retractions are shown, not silently overwritten. The seam of the mend stays visible.

    Revision log (element 9 + 8, enacted)

  9. The revisable, unfinished object

    The reading room is a draft that stays a draft. Prior states remain legible beneath revisions, as record — see the version tag in the masthead and the log above.

  10. Mutual recognition

    Writers appear as full people, with their nations, communities, and situated voices — never as logos or ranked authorities.

  11. Situated, anti-extractive materiality

    Palette and type are place- and subject-derived, not a universal template; the design refuses both the corporate default and the militant cliché.

    See the colophon for why these specific two inks — and why the palette is offered as place-provisional, not as a recommendation for anyone else.

  12. Governance as form — the map replaces the manifesto (here, literally)

    Navigation is a map and a set of questions, not a marketing funnel; the document exposes its own workings as you read.

    These margin marks are the aesthetic naming itself. That is element 12, running.

05 — multimedia

More than one way to receive a text.

Orality is a relational and an access commitment, not an add-on. Every reading has a spoken version; nothing autoplays; everything is captioned and transcribed. The slots below mark where recorded media live; the map above and the ambient field are the media already running on the page.

06 — strategy

How to grow a reading site without dominating anyone.

A site against domination cannot recruit through domination. The engagement playbook — infinite scroll, streaks, autoplay, push notifications, behavioural analytics — is exactly the colonial grammar the framework refuses, aimed at the reader's attention. So the strategy is built from the opposite instincts.

  • Invitation over capture. No infinite feed, no autoplay, no streaks or badges, no notifications engineered to pull you back.
  • No surveillance. No trackers, no behavioural analytics, no attention metrics. Opacity is a reader's right too.
  • Slow reading as care. Honest reading-time estimates (so you can choose), inline annotation, and clean print and offline versions.
  • Pathways, not funnels. Several true entry points — the refusal, a single text, a whole track — each leading outward to the writers.
  • Reading circles and assemblies. Prompts and printable kits for reading together, because DIY is not solitary (§15).
  • Printable zines. The manifesto and each track as a risograph-ready booklet to photocopy and hand on, attribution intact.
  • Point outward. Prominent links to authors, publishers, and communities. The aim is to make the site unnecessary.

Element 12 / §15: refusing growth-hacking is not a limitation bolted on — it is the strategy. The restraint is the message.

Circulation & consent

The site spreads the way a zine spreads: hand to hand, forkable, reciprocal. Adapt it, translate it, reprint it — with attribution, and never by extracting Indigenous cultural material without the relevant communities' permission. Consented circulation, not open appropriation; DIY, but never alone.
