A Relational Sovereignty Manifesto

This is not a dating system. This is not a softer vocabulary for control. This is a refusal.

A refusal of the relationship as default. A refusal of the lover as posession. A refusal of the child as extension. A refusal of the friend as resource. A refusal of the caregiver as servant. A refusal of love organized like an empire.
I do not own you. You do not own me. We are still responsible to each other. That is the contradiction. Live inside it.

  1. 01 · Love is not a land claim

    Colonialism drew borders around land, bodies, and the home: my wife, my children, my country. Mine. This grammar isn't innocent. The ideology of settler colonialism made land and people into property and rendered certain forms of kinship legally void. Property law, racial rule, marriage law, and inherited family norms still shape who may claim whom and whose kinship counts. This is the reminder that no one becomes your property because they kissed you, married you, raised you, or once promised to stay.

    Love is participation, not annexation.

  2. 02 · Burn the default settings

    Many relationships arrive with rules already attached: couple first, blood first, men command, women care, parents know, children obey. We will not mistake tradition for consent, or familiarity for freedom. That being said, a relationship chosen inside inherited law, kinship, or ceremony isn't less free than one built from nothing. Freedom can be practised through inherited law, kinship, and ceremony as well as individual choice. Every relationship must answer for itself: who made its rules, who benefits, who can leave without losing everything?

    Ask who can question the rule, change it, and survive refusing it.

  3. 03 · Consent is not a colonial permit

    Consent is not a visa granting permanent access to a body, a life, a future. Consent is alive. It can be withdrawn, renegotiated, given in one part of a relationship and not another. Yes to touch is not yes to sex. Yes yesterday is not yes forever. Consent without the ability to refuse is performative.

    Ask who can say no and what saying no will cost.

  4. 04 · Autonomy is not isolation

    The empire loves the ruler who needs nobody and the worker who receives nothing. We reject both. The problem is not dependence (we all depend) but dependence controlled by someone else. Relational freedom requires meaningful influence over the conditions of interdependence.

    Independence should not cost dignity.

  5. 05 · No hierarchy without accountability

    Not every relationship is symmetrical; parent and child, teacher and student, carer and cared-for hold different power. Difference is real, but domination is not inevitable. Relationships require authority to be named, limited, and contestable. Power that cannot be challenged becomes rule.

    No secret thrones.

  6. 06 · Build constellations, not pyramids

    Romance is not automatically the highest relationship. Marriage is not automatically deeper than friendship. Blood is not automatically safer than chosen kin. Value each bond by what it actually holds (trust, care, history, risk, labour) not by the rank that the state, church, or market has assigned it.

    Grow rhizomes and build constellations, not pyramids.

  7. 07 · Make the invisible labour visible

    Colonial relationships survive on hidden work: someone else remembers the appointments, calms the conflict, explains the racism, absorbs the anger, disappears so the peace can appear. We choose to name the work, count it, and redistribute it. Relational justice encourages rest.

    Care is work. Care is power. Care must not become a caste system.

  8. 08 · Practise the right to opacity

    You are not entitled to total access, to every memory, password, thought, or sacred teaching. Colonial power demands that people become completely legible: explain, confess, classify. We refuse. Intimacy does not require total exposure; trust does not require surveillance.

    Some parts of a person are beyond our grasp and comprehension.

  9. 09 · Refusal is a relational act

    No. Not like this. Not today. This does not belong to you. I am changing the agreement. Refusal can make a bond more honest; it can also end a bond that has become harmful. Obedience is not the same as loyalty, fear is not commitment, nor silence peace.

    A relationship that cannot survive a boundary was surviving on access, not love.

  10. 10 · Liberation is material

    You cannot decolonize a relationship through language alone. A beautiful agreement means little when one person controls the housing, the money, the healthcare, the immigration status, the access to children. Emotional freedom requires material alternatives. Mutual aid is part of consent.

    A person is not free to leave when leaving means losing everything.

  11. 11 · Relationships are not private empires

    What happens between two people does not always stay between two people; children, friends, communities, care networks, and land absorb the consequences. We reject the excuse that anything is ethical because consenting adults arranged it behind closed doors. We are accountable to those that absorb the consequences (not the state or crowds with an opinion).

    No private freedom built on public damage.

  12. 12 · Repair is not rebranding

    An apology is not repair. A therapy word is not repair. Repair requires changed behaviour, time, returning what was taken, and surrendering the benefits produced by harm. Nobody owes reconciliation to the person who harmed them. Nobody owes access as proof of healing.

    Accountability without consequences is just a public relations maneuver.

  13. 13 · Let relationships change shape

    The colonial imagination loves fixed categories (friend, partner, spouse, ex) and demands every bond stay recognizable. We refuse the museum. Friendship may become love; love may become kinship; marriage may become co-parenting. And an ending does not invalidate the past.

    We do not have to destroy what was real in order to accept what is over.

  14. 14 · Regeneration, not endurance

    Colonial relationships reproduce by insisting there is no alternative: this is just how men are, just what marriage is, just human nature. We are not here only to outlast damaged forms of connection. We are here to make new ones. We will make kinship after displacement, trust after betrayal, family beyond blood. We do not worship destruction; we destroy what prevents life, then we build.

    There is a future, and it will be relational.

  15. 15 · Do it yourself, but never alone

    Do not wait for the state to recognize your relationships, the market to package your liberation, or an app to optimize your intimacy. Write your agreements. Build your care networks. Make caregiving public. Make accountability ordinary. Print the zine. Walk away when necessary; return when invited.

    DIY does not mean solitary. It means we stop waiting for the institutions that harmed us to teach us how to love.

  16. 16 · The house is not a rehearsal

    We will not build a world without police, borders, and extraction while running small versions of all three at home. This isn't because the private is where politics begins (it is not, and treating it that way has kept a great many people busy and harmless). Home and movement are connected sites of power, but they are not interchangeable. Work against domination in both, and a movement that cannot survive its own kitchen will not survive anything else.

    Do both. Neither one is waiting for the other to finish.

Our demands

Relationships without ownership. Consent with material alternatives. Care without compulsory self-sacrifice. Authority that can be questioned. Privacy without suspicion. Commitment without possession. Community without forced conformity. Accountability without carceral revenge. Kinship beyond blood and romance. The right to change, to refuse, to remain opaque, to leave, and to return differently.

The pact

I will not treat love as ownership. I will not confuse access with intimacy. I will not demand transparency as proof of loyalty. I will examine the power I hold and make hidden labour visible. I will respect refusal, and I will not use care to create debt. I will not call domination tradition, possession commitment, or silence harmony. I will help build the conditions in which others can freely choose. I will remain responsible for the consequences of my freedom. I will practise connection without conquest. I will love without colonizing.

No masters. No property. No disposable people. No colonies in our relationships.
