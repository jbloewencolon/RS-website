What this is not

Four limits, stated before anything else.

Not radical individualism

It does not mean doing whatever one wants, refusing all obligations, treating people as interchangeable, or using therapeutic language to escape accountability. People are interdependent. Freedom exists through relationships, resources, care, community, and material support.

Not a synonym for relationship anarchy

Relationship anarchy is an important influence, particularly its critique of compulsory hierarchy and romantic supremacy. Relational sovereignty adds material dependency, collective responsibility, power differences, caregiving, dependants, land, and culturally specific protocols.

Not a substitute for decolonization

Interpersonal transformation does not replace Indigenous jurisdiction, land return, political self-determination, cultural resurgence, or material restitution. Decolonization is not better communication, personal healing, alternative dating, or lifestyle experimentation.

Not one universal Indigenous philosophy

Indigenous nations hold distinct laws, teachings, kinship systems, territories, languages, and protocols. Relationality, reciprocity, survivance, and sovereignty are not interchangeable spiritual principles. Sources here are named by author, nation, and intellectual tradition.

One clarification that belongs beside these four, not folded into any of them: chosen monogamy is not a lesser or unexamined form of this framework's promise, and this site does not treat non-monogamy as more evolved. What it does not protect is coercion running the other way — being pressured, guilt-tripped, or worn down into opening a relationship you did not want opened, sometimes in this exact vocabulary. Wanting one partner, on your own terms, is not a pathology this framework is trying to cure.

⌖ Safety — relationship exercises can be dangerous where coercive control or violence is present. Joint communication tools are not appropriate when one participant is violent, coercive, retaliatory, or controlling. See the notices beside each tool.

And the fifth limit, about this website

A website returns no land.

This site refuses to reproduce colonial form — no trackers, no third parties, no consent theatre, no attempt to keep you here. That is anti-colonial design, which is a real but modest achievement. It is not decolonization, and sufficiently thoughtful design never will be.

Where this work touches actual sovereignty it is jurisdictional: whose law governs a community's data, on whose territory the servers sit, who holds deletion authority, who may crawl and train. The substrate below states what is known, what is not, and where this build currently fails.

Territory this site doesn't know how to name, on the device you're reading this from, is not something a website should guess at either — native-land.ca is a starting point, not a legal record; the organisation says so itself, and its map stays a work in progress rather than a finished boundary line. Nothing from it is embedded or reproduced here — this is a link out, not a lookup built in.

Type, and why it changed

Version 0.1 loaded three faces from a font CDN. That disclosed every reader's address to a third party on page load, while the footer claimed no third parties. The claim was false, so the fonts went.

What replaced them is a system stack: no request leaves your device for type, and the faces already on your machine carry far wider orthographic coverage than the display faces did. The identity is quieter. That is the correct trade.

Display · ui-sans-serif, system-ui, Helvetica Neue, Helvetica, Arial
Reading · Georgia, Iowan Old Style, Palatino Linotype, Palatino
Structure · ui-monospace, SF Mono, Cascadia Mono, Menlo, Consolas

Coverage, checked not assumed

A font that turns a nation's name into boxes makes a people unwritable in their own name. Every diacritic this site carries was measured against the notdef box in each stack, rather than trusted.

Re-measured 2026 with a pixel-comparison check: the previous method compared advance widths against the .notdef box, which cannot detect a missing glyph in a monospace face at all — every character there shares one fixed advance width by design, present or not. That was a real gap in "checked, not assumed," not a platform-coverage gap, and it's fixed now. Confirmed clean this session on Chrome / Linux, the one platform available to re-verify it on. Windows, macOS, iOS, and Android — and Safari, Firefox, and Edge — have not been independently re-checked and are a known gap. Results can vary by platform, which is why each stack names several faces. The check itself is viewable and re-runnable.

⌖ The caveat that governs all of this: opacity is owed to the crawler and the market, never to the disabled reader. Nothing here is hidden from assistive technology, and no semantic structure is removed to look less legible. Withhold from extraction; disclose fully to those who need access to participate.

Protocol-gated access — where a community, not this project, sets who may see what and under which relationship — is not built yet. When it is, it will follow work that already exists rather than a scheme invented here: Mukurtu CMS, built with the Warumungu community; Local Contexts TK and BC Labels; and the CARE Principles for Indigenous data governance.

Labour and money

Who has worked on this

At v0.2: one unpaid person, writing and building. Every future contributor, translator, reviewer, and maintainer will be named here individually, not aggregated into a credit line.

What is paid

Nothing yet, which is why the archive's source notes are marked provisional. Indigenous review, disability-access review, and facilitation are paid work; until there is money for them, they are listed as owed rather than described as done.

What it costs to run

Static pages, no database, no analytics, no CDN. Once hosted, the running cost and its funding source will be published as a figure here.

Sources this site is built from

Four research documents and a manifesto held by the project, and the writers named in the archive — who are owed the credit, and to whom this site is only a door.

The limit

A website returns no land.

Everything on this page is more accurately called anti-colonial design: a refusal to reproduce colonial form. That is a real but modest achievement, and it is not the repatriation of territory or life. Sufficiently thoughtful design is not decolonization.

The substrate is not exited either — a naming registry, two browser engines, corporate networks, and a physical data centre remain. Where this work touches actual sovereignty it is jurisdictional: whose law governs a community's data, on whose territory the servers sit, who holds deletion authority, who may crawl and train. That is a question about land, not layout.
