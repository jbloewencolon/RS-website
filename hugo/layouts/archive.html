Read the people, not the summary.

No single source contains this whole framework. It is assembled from Indigenous political thought, feminist relational autonomy, disability justice, care ethics, and transformative-justice practice — each with its own authors, nations, and arguments.

Every entry links away from here, to the author, the publisher, or the open-access copy. Where no stable public link exists, the holder is named instead of a guess. This site is a door; its success is how quickly you stop needing it.

⌖ Indigenous relationality, sovereignty, refusal, and survivance are not a generic radical style. These traditions are nation-specific, and borrowing them as atmosphere reproduces colonial extraction even when it calls itself rebellion. Held in common

A shelf is a cadastre: one parcel per book, no overlaps, every text assigned to exactly one owner. Some of these texts refuse that. The four in the centre below are claimed fully by both traditions at once — not shared out, not split, not filed under one and cross-referenced from the other. Two jurisdictions, one holding.

Reading order is left to right; on a narrow screen this diagram scrolls sideways. The three lists below are titled for anyone not reading them by their visual position — this is a Venn diagram made of real list text, not an image, so nothing here needs a separate text alternative.

Opacity as archive design

Absence does not mean the archive is incomplete.

Most archives assume more access is always better. This one will hold seven access states, with consent as a metadata field rather than an afterthought. Sometimes withholding is part of ethical preservation. None of it is built yet, and the model it will follow already exists — Mukurtu, Local Contexts, CARE.
